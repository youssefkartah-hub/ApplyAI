Safran Aerosystems

Engineering intern, Fluids & Fuel Systems

Jun to Aug 2024Morocco

  • Assisted with production and acceptance testing of kerosene temperature probes and fuel-gauging devices: about 110 tests across 6 part numbers.
  • Standardized the MATLAB and Excel test logs and the traveler fields, saving about 3 minutes of data entry per test, 5 to 8% of the test.
  • Worked with production staff to update work instructions, checklists and margin-of-safety checks, which led to about 10 fewer repeat-test and hold tags.
  • Compiled root-cause data packs within 24 to 48 hours of a failure (test traces, as-built against spec, photos). They helped close 2 nonconformance reports.

Nittany Rocket Labs

Structures team, Penn State's student rocketry team

Sep 2025 to presentTeam

  • With the structures team, lay up, vacuum bag, cure, demold and trim the rocket's carbon-fiber body tubes and flat plates.
  • Modeled parts in SolidWorks for manufacturability, with fasteners and GD&T, and checked fit with 3D-printed and laser-cut prototypes before any carbon was cut.

About

How I work

{{pic src="portrait.jpg" alt="Portrait of Youssef Kartah" sizes="300px"}}

I am a senior in aerospace engineering at Penn State, on the autonomous vehicles track, graduating in May 2027.

Most of what I know about drones I learned by buying the parts myself and getting them to fly. My habits come from there: settle the trade study before spending the money, keep a manual override on anything that flies itself, and tune from the flight log instead of from how the flight felt.

A summer in production test at Safran Aerosystems showed me why aerospace procedures are as strict as they are. It also showed me something about myself. I went out of my way to ask my supervisor for feedback only three times in three months, and I should have asked far more often.
